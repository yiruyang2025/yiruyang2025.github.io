## Some Notes

<br>

**ZEISS Ophthalmology Digital Twin Workflow**


```
[Step 1] Data Acquisition  
   - OCT (Optical Coherence Tomography)  
   - Fundus Photography  
   - Biometry (corneal thickness, curvature, lens data)  
   - Patient History & Clinical Records  

        ↓

[Step 2] AI-Based Reconstruction  
   - Segmentation of anatomical structures (cornea, retina, optic nerve)  
   - 3D geometry reconstruction from multimodal images  
   - Building a patient-specific virtual eye  

        ↓

[Step 3] Digital Twin Simulation  
   - Disease progression modeling  
     (e.g., glaucoma nerve fiber loss, AMD lesion growth)  
   - Surgical outcome prediction  
     (e.g., LASIK corneal reshaping, IOL implantation optics)  
   - Biomechanics & optics simulation  

        ↓

[Step 4] Clinical Decision Support  
   - Personalized surgery planning  
   - Risk assessment & treatment optimization  
   - Doctor–patient communication (visual and interactive)  

        ↓

[Step 5] Continuous Update  
   - Post-surgery follow-up data integration  
   - AI re-trains/refines the patient’s twin  
   - Establishes a lifelong “virtual patient”  
```

| **2015** | *Immersed boundary-finite element model of fluid-structure interaction in the aortic root* (Griffith et al.)                                                                                   | Combines IB method with finite-element hyperelastic modeling of the aortic root and valve; reproduces realistic cardiac output and valve mechanics.                                                             |

| **2023** | *Comparison of Immersed Boundary Simulations of Heart Valve Hemodynamics against In Vitro 4D Flow MRI Data* (Kaiser et al.)                                                                    | Validates IB simulations against in vitro 4D flow MRI of a pulmonary valve; shows strong qualitative and quantitative agreement.                                                                                |
