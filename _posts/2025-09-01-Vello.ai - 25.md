## Topics

**The historical progression is: inheritance → DNA structure and copying → sequencing → mutation mechanisms → functional interpretation.**

| **Core question** | **Nobel foundation** | **Frontier research** | **Potential and current limits** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Could a protein-altering variant be harmful?** | **1968 · Physiology or Medicine** — Robert W. Holley, Har Gobind Khorana and Marshall W. Nirenberg: interpretation of the genetic code and its role in protein synthesis. | **Google DeepMind · AlphaMissense:** predicts the potential pathogenicity of **missense variants**, which change a protein’s amino-acid sequence. | Helps prioritise variants for investigation. **A prediction is neither experimental proof nor a fetal diagnosis**, and missense predictions do not cover every variant class. ([www.nobelprize.org][1]) |
| **How could a variant disrupt gene regulation or RNA splicing?** | **1965 · Physiology or Medicine** — François Jacob, André Lwoff and Jacques Monod: genetic regulation. **1993 · Physiology or Medicine** — Richard J. Roberts and Phillip A. Sharp: split genes. | **Google DeepMind · AlphaGenome:** predicts sequence-dependent molecular features and variant effects, including **gene expression, splicing and chromatin-related features**, across coding and noncoding regions. | Supports mechanistic interpretation of variants. **A predicted molecular effect does not establish disease, penetrance or clinical severity.** The model is not validated or approved for clinical use. ([www.nobelprize.org][1]) |
| **Which genome-wide variants deserve investigation first?** | **1980 · Chemistry** — Paul Berg: recombinant-DNA research; Walter Gilbert and Frederick Sanger: nucleic-acid sequencing methods. | **Google DeepMind · AlphaGenome Atlas — September 2026:** precomputed molecular-effect predictions for approximately **9 billion possible single-nucleotide variants**. The **AlphaGenome Variant Impact (AVI) score** integrates AlphaGenome and AlphaMissense predictions. | Enables prioritisation of coding and noncoding variants. **These are possible substitutions and predicted effects—not 9 billion observed or proven pathogenic variants.** The resource does not replace clinical variant classification. ([www.nobelprize.org][2]) |
| **What molecular structures and interactions could explain biological effects?** | **2024 · Chemistry** — David Baker: computational protein design; Demis Hassabis and John Jumper: protein-structure prediction. | **Google DeepMind / Isomorphic Labs · AlphaFold 3:** predicts structures of complexes involving **proteins, DNA, RNA, ligands and other molecular components**. | Supports mechanistic hypotheses and drug discovery. **Structure prediction alone does not establish whether a variant is pathogenic or how it affects a fetus.** The Nobel recognition should not be described as an award specifically for AlphaFold 3. ([www.nobelprize.org][3]) |
| **How can literature, genomic databases and computational analyses be connected?** | **No single corresponding Nobel Prize:** this direction combines molecular biology, statistics, computing and scientific methodology. | **Anthropic · Claude Science — June 2026:** a scientific workbench connecting literature, databases and computational tools, with **traceable code, outputs and analysis histories**. | Can accelerate evidence synthesis and reproducible analysis. **It is a research-workflow platform, not a dedicated fetal pathogenicity model.** Traceability supports review but does not guarantee correctness. ([www.anthropic.com][4]) |
| **How can AI-generated biological hypotheses be tested experimentally?** | **2007 · Physiology or Medicine** — Mario R. Capecchi, Martin J. Evans and Oliver Smithies: targeted genetic modification in mice using embryonic stem cells. **2020 · Chemistry** — Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer A. Doudna: a genome-editing method. | **Anthropic’s life-sciences research:** combines AI with experimental biology. Claude Science supports workflows including **CRISPR screen design and biological-data analysis**. | Could strengthen the **prediction → experiment → revision** cycle. **A cellular or animal-model effect does not automatically establish human fetal disease or predict its severity.** ([www.nobelprize.org][5]) |
